Customer Voice Radar: what should Zepto fix first?

App store reviews are the largest free source of customer feedback, and nobody reads five thousand of them. I built a tool that does, then used it to rank what a quick-commerce team should fix first.

Type
Personal project. I have no connection to Zepto and used only public reviews.
Data
5,336 Play Store reviews, 10 Sep to 30 Oct 2026
Output
20 ranked problems and 3 recommended fixes, each with a success measure
5,336reviews read
20distinct problems found
47 of 48quotes verified word for word

The question

A product team with a long list of complaints needs to know three things about each one: how many users it affects, how angry it makes them, and whether it is getting worse. Star ratings answer none of these, and reading reviews by hand does not scale.

What I did

  • Collected the newest 5,336 reviews. 3,645 of them were rated one to three stars.
  • Grouped the low-rated reviews by meaning, across English, Hindi and Hinglish, so the same complaint in two languages lands in one group.
  • Named each group the way a product team would, using an AI model, and separated problems a team can act on from general venting.
  • Ranked each problem by size, by average rating, and by trend: its share of recent reviews compared with its share of older ones.

What the reviews say

The five largest problems, out of 20:

Two smaller problems were growing fastest: handling fees charged despite "zero handling fee" advertising, and orders cancelled after a long wait with no way for the customer to cancel or change the address.

What I would fix first, and how I would know it worked

1. Photo proof of delivery, and instant refunds for missing items

The rider photographs the open bag at handover. A claim the photo does not disprove is refunded automatically, and a disputed one goes to a person. This targets the largest problem, which is still growing.

Measure: the share of missing-item claims rejected without showing the customer a delivery photo.

2. Photo claims and automatic refunds for spoiled food, plus an expiry check at packing

This targets about 790 reviews about rotten, expired or melted items.

Measure: the median time from complaint to refund or replacement.

3. An honest delivery time, and one-tap cancel when an order is late

This targets 303 reviews about wrong delivery times and 120 about orders cancelled after long waits.

Measure: the share of orders delivered within five minutes of the time shown at checkout.

What I decided, and why

Rank by three things, not one

The biggest problem is not always the most urgent. Ranking by size, severity and trend together is what surfaced the handling-fee complaint: small today, but growing faster than anything else.

Never show a quote the AI might have invented

Every quote the AI model picked is checked word for word against the real reviews. 47 of 48 problem quotes passed, and the one that did not was dropped. A recommendation backed by a made-up quote is worse than no recommendation.

Check the tool by hand before trusting it

I built a small labelling screen to check the tool's answers against my own. The first round showed that about a third of reviews fitted none of the 11 problems the first version had found. Regrouping more finely raised that to 20 problems, and added rude delivery partners, payment failures, fake products and handling fees.

Limits I would state upfront

  • Seven weeks of data. The Play Store only returns recent reviews, so "trend" compares the second half of that window with the first. It cannot show long-term change.
  • Accuracy is not measured yet. The hand-check is built but not finished, so I do not claim an accuracy number.
  • Counts are approximate. Many reviews did not fit a group cleanly and were assigned to the nearest one, which inflates group sizes.
  • Reviewers are not a random sample. Angry customers write more reviews. The ranking compares problems with each other. It does not say what share of all customers is affected.

What I would do next

  • Finish the hand-check and publish an accuracy figure.
  • Run the same analysis on two competitors, to separate problems every quick-commerce app has from problems specific to one.
  • Rerun it monthly, so trend means months and not weeks.
